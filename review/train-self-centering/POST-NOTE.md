Read verbatim 2026-11-27: Wikipedia "Wheelset (rail transport)": "Most train wheels have a conical taper of about 1 in 20 to enable the wheelset to follow curves..."; "The cone increases the effective diameter of the wheel as it moves towards the outer rail, and since the wheels are mounted rigidly on the axle, the outer wheels travel slightly farther, causing the wheelsets to more efficiently follow the curve."; "Not all railroads have employed conical-tread wheels" (BART used cylindrical wheels until 2016). The straight-track self-centring (a sideways drift puts the wheel on its wider part, which pulls ahead and steers the axle back) is the standard conicity mechanism of wheelset dynamics; the Wikipedia page covers the curve case and mentions oscillation at high speed, so do not quote the page for the straight-track part.
Not claimed: any speed, any exact taper on screen, that every metro uses cones (the caption says "most").

Extra facts used (same page, 2026-11-27): "Abnormal wear at the wheel-rail interface is thus avoided, along with the loud, piercing, very high-pitched squeal ... flanges grinding along the rail"; BART "built with cylindrical wheels and flat-topped rails, started to re-profile the wheels in 2016 with conical treads after years of complaints about the squeal"; "if the degree of conicality is inappropriate for the suspension and track, an unpleasant oscillation can occur at high speeds".

## Instagram caption
A metro has no steering wheel. So how does it stay on the track? 🚆 One mechanism a day.
Most train wheels are not flat. They are a very slight cone, about 1 in 20, wider on the inside. Both wheels sit on one rigid axle, so they turn together. If the train drifts sideways, one wheel rides on a wider part of its cone, covers more distance every turn and steers the axle back toward the centre. On a curve the outer wheel rides wider and covers the longer path, so the cone does the steering. Without it, the flange grinds on the rail and the wheels squeal. Too much cone can make the train wobble at high speed.
Double tap if you knew this. Save it. Follow for one mechanism a day.
