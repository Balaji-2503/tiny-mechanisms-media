## Instagram caption
A metro has no steering wheel. So how does it stay on the track? 🚆 One mechanism a day.
Most train wheels are not flat. They are a very slight cone, wider on the inside. Both wheels sit on one rigid axle, so they always turn together. If the train drifts sideways, one wheel rides on a wider part of its cone, covers more distance every turn and steers the axle back toward the centre. On a curve the outer wheel rides wider and covers the longer path, so the cone does the steering. The flange on the inside edge is only a backup.
