## Instagram caption
Power lines carry up to 765,000 volts. Your home uses 230 (120 in North America). Why so high? ⚡
Why power lines use high voltage: power = voltage × current, and a wire's heat loss grows with the square of the current. Raise the voltage 10× and the current drops 10×, so the loss drops 100×. A real 160 km line carrying 1,000 MW loses about 4.2% at 345 kV but only 0.5–1.1% at 765 kV.
How the power grid works: transformers step the voltage up at the station and back down near your home. And past a point the air itself leaks power (corona), which is why big lines run bundles of wires.
