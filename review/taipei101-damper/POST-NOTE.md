## Instagram caption
A 660-tonne steel ball hangs inside this skyscraper, and it's supposed to move. 🏙️
How Taipei 101's damper works: strong wind makes a tall tower sway, which is uncomfortable for people inside and hard on the glass. So engineers hung a giant pendulum from the 92nd floor down to the 88th: 41 steel plates welded into a 5.5 m ball. Its swing is tuned to the tower's own sway, so when the building leans one way, the heavy ball lags and pulls back the other way, soaking up the motion.
In 2015, Typhoon Soudelor swung it about 1 m, its biggest movement ever recorded. Two smaller 6-tonne dampers sit in the spire, and visitors can watch the big one work.
