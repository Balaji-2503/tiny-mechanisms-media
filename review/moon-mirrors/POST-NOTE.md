## Instagram caption
There are mirrors on the Moon, and they show it's drifting away from us: about 3.8 cm every year. 🌕
In 1969 the Apollo 11 astronauts left a panel of 100 small glass corner cubes. A corner cube works like the inside corner of a box: light bounces off three faces at right angles and goes straight back the way it came.
Observatories fire laser pulses at it. By the time the beam reaches the Moon it's several kilometres wide, and only a trickle of light comes back, sometimes about one photon every few seconds. The round trip takes about 2.5 seconds; time it, multiply by the speed of light, halve it, and you have the distance, today to within about a millimetre.
