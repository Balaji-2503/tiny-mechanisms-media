## Instagram caption
Your physics textbook may have the Tacoma Narrows collapse wrong. 🌉 It wasn't just resonance.
The Tacoma Narrows Bridge opened on July 1, 1940. Its deck was so slender and flexible that it bounced up and down in the wind, earning it the nickname "Galloping Gertie". On November 7, 1940, in winds of about 64 km/h, the deck began to twist, bigger and bigger, until it broke apart.
Why it wasn't simple resonance: resonance is gusts pushing at just the right rhythm, like pushing a swing. Tacoma was aeroelastic flutter: once the deck twisted, the twist changed how the wind hit it, and the wind made the twist bigger. Above about 56 km/h the motion fed itself.
What changed: later bridges used open trusses instead of solid girders, and wind-tunnel tests became mandatory.
