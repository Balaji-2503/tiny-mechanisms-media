## Instagram caption
A second is exactly 9,192,631,770 waves from an atom. ⏱️ Here's why we stopped trusting the Earth.
What is a second? For a long time it was 1/86,400 of an average day, but Earth's spin varies and is slowing ever so slightly. So the second was tied to the length of a year, and then, in 1967, to an atom: caesium-133.
How an atomic clock works: a caesium atom has two energy levels very close together, and microwaves at exactly the right frequency flip it between them. The clock locks its microwaves onto that frequency and counts the waves. Count 9,192,631,770 of them and that's one second, by definition. Because Earth's spin wanders, leap seconds were added at irregular intervals; today clocks worldwide "vote" on the time, and newer atomic clocks are about 100× more precise.
