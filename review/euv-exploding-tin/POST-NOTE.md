## Instagram caption
The machine that prints the world's most advanced computer chips makes its light by blasting drops of molten tin. 💥 50,000 times a second.
How computer chips are made: chips are printed with light through a pattern, like a stencil, and shorter light prints smaller details. EUV lithography uses 13.5 nm light. Each tin drop (about 25 µm wide, flying at 70 m/s) is hit by a weak laser pulse that flattens it into a pancake, then a strong pulse turns it into a glowing plasma.
Most materials absorb this light, so there are no lenses: it travels through a vacuum and bounces off mirrors with 100+ thin layers, polished smoother than one atom. Scale a mirror up to the size of Germany and its tallest bump would be 1 mm.
