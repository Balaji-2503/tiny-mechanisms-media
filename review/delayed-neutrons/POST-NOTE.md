## Instagram caption
Nuclear reactors can be controlled because of neutrons that show up late. ⚛️ Fewer than 1 in 100 of them.
When a uranium atom splits, it fires out a few neutrons almost instantly, and those split more atoms: the chain reaction. If every neutron came instantly, the power could change far faster than anything could respond. That runaway state has a name: prompt critical.
But for uranium-235, about 0.65% of neutrons come late. Some fission fragments decay first and only then release a neutron, from milliseconds to minutes later. Reactors are run just short of critical on the instant neutrons alone, and the late ones make up the difference, so power changes over seconds instead. In one textbook example, the same small push makes power rise about 30 times slower with them: a period of 14 seconds instead of under half a second.
Like balancing a bike: it works because your reflexes are faster than the wobble.
