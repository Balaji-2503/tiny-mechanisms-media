## Instagram caption
Five sheets of plastic, each thinner than a human hair, keep the Webb telescope colder than −220 °C while the Sun shines on the other side. ☀️❄️
How Webb's sunshield works: Webb sees infrared light, which is essentially heat, so the telescope must be very cold or its own warmth would drown the signal. Its tennis-court-sized sunshield is five layers of Kapton coated with aluminium; the Sun-facing layer is just 0.05 mm thick and the others are half that.
Why five thin layers instead of one thick one? Each layer is cooler than the one below, heat radiates out from between them, and the vacuum in each gap insulates. The gaps are wider at the edges, so heat escapes sideways into space. Modelled temperatures: about 110 °C on the Sun side, about −237 °C on the cold side.
